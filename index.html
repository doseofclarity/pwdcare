Overview & Magnitude of Disability

A person with disability (PWD) is anyone who has a physical, mental, or sensory impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities. In the Philippines, disability is a significant public health concern.

📊 WHO Estimates: 10% of any developing country's population is physically disabled. Only 2-3% have access to rehabilitation services.
🇵🇭 Philippines (1997 NSO): 549,062 registered PWDs. Breakdown by age: 53% productive age (15-59), 30% elderly (60+), 17% young (0-14).
Challenge: 96 rehabilitation centers are concentrated in urban areas, while 70% of Filipinos live in rural areas → majority of PWDs in rural communities lack access to rehab services.

Legal Bases for PWD Care

  • Republic Act No. 7277 (1992) – “Magna Carta for Disabled Persons”. Provides for rehabilitation, self-development, self-reliance, and integration of PWDs into mainstream society. Mandates accessibility, non-discrimination, and support services.
  • Department Order No. 182-c s. 1992 – Operationalized Community-Based Rehabilitation Program (CBRP) after successful pilot areas. Institutionalized CBRP in the public health system.
  • 1987 Philippine Constitution, Article XIII, Section 13 – State shall establish a special agency for disabled persons for rehabilitation, self-development and self-reliance.
  • UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (ratified 2008) – Philippines committed to inclusive community-based services.
Key principle of RA 7277: “Disabled persons are part of society, thus they shall be helped to become self-reliant and productive.”

Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR)

CBR is a creative application of the Primary Health Care approach in rehabilitation services for PWDs. It involves measures taken at the community level to use and build on the resources of the community — including PWDs themselves, families, and the community as a whole.

🎯 Goal: Improvement of quality of life and productivity of disabled/handicapped persons.
📝 Objective: Reduce prevalence of disability through prevention, early detection, and provision of rehabilitation services at community level.
🧩 CBR Components (DOH Order 182-c):
  • Social Preparation: Site identification, committee organization, training of local supervisors, community survey, advocacy.
  • Service Preparation: Family care rehabilitation, provision of technical aids (braces, prosthesis, assistive devices), essential surgery, drugs.
  • Training & Manpower Development: Capacity building for community health workers and family caregivers.
  • IEC & Counseling: Information campaigns to reduce stigma, family counseling.
  • Monitoring, Supervision & Evaluation.

Prevention & Early Detection

The Public Health Nurse plays a crucial role in the three levels of prevention for disabilities:

Primary Prevention
Immunization (polio, measles, rubella), maternal nutrition, prenatal care, safe delivery, injury prevention, genetic counseling.
Secondary Prevention
Newborn screening, early developmental screening, vision/hearing tests, identification of children with delayed milestones, prompt treatment of infections.
Tertiary Prevention
Rehabilitation, assistive devices, physical therapy, occupational therapy, social integration, prevention of secondary complications.
Early detection tools: Growth monitoring, Denver Developmental Screening Test (DDST) adaptation, hearing/vision screening in schools, home visits for high-risk infants.

PHN Role in PWD Care

  • Case finding & registration: Identify PWDs during home visits, community surveys, and well-baby clinics. Maintain a PWD registry at the RHU.
  • Assessment & referral: Conduct functional assessment; refer to physicians, physical therapists, or specialty centers (e.g., orthopedic, eye, ear).
  • Family education & counseling: Teach families how to provide basic rehabilitation (range of motion exercises, positioning, skin care for bedridden). Address psychological and social stigma.
  • Coordination with CBR partners: Work with BNSs, BHWs, PT/OT, social workers, DSWD, LGUs and NGOs like Philippine Council for the Deaf, APCD.
  • Assistive device support: Facilitate provision of wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, eyeglasses (e.g., eyeglasses bank program).
  • Advocacy: Promote barrier-free environment, accessible health facilities, and inclusion of PWDs in community activities (e.g., BHW training, livelihood programs).
  • Monitoring & evaluation: Track progress of PWDs under CBR, assess functional improvement, and evaluate family coping.

Rehabilitation & Empowerment

Rehabilitation aims to enable PWDs to achieve optimal physical, mental, and social functioning. CBR empowers PWDs to become active contributors to society.

🦽 Types of Rehabilitation:
• Physical (PT, OT, speech)
• Medical (surgery, prosthetics)
• Social (counseling, community reintegration)
• Vocational (skills training, livelihood)
💪 Empowerment Strategies:
• Self-help groups / PWD organizations
• Skills training (e.g., computer, crafts)
• Access to education & employment (RA 7277 quota: 1% of positions in government reserved for PWDs)
• Political participation (PWD representatives in local councils)
Community integration: Barrier-free buildings, accessible transport, sign language interpreters, inclusive health services, and anti-stigma campaigns.
